# Play Clipper — clip rules (every wrong clip → a rule; never remove)
v9 = current file: new scoreboard-first clip flow (Roadmap Phase 0) replaces the old matcher's patch rules below; v8 kept as the old version.
Enforced in snap check (fixDone version bump re-runs it) + audit() self-check after every scan (red dot on flagged plays, count in status pill).
1. Clip may start at a camera cut only if the cut is ≥1.5s before the snap (else full 3s lead-in) — FSU play 3 started after the handoff.
2. Clock frozen through the clip (−0.5s → +8s same reading) = not a live play → move to next spot where clock starts from ESPN's time — FSU clip 15 sat on the extra point. If the clock is already running before the snap (restarted on the ready signal after a pre-snap penalty) there's no "starts" moment → back-solve: snap = read time + read clock − ESPN clock (≥2 agreeing reads) — FSU defense play 20 (Q1 0:28, after illegal snap) sat on frozen 0:44.
3. A play must start ≥8s after the previous play's snap (else it's a replay of it) — FSU clip 16.
4. OT has no clock → match by scoreboard down & distance in order.
5. Long gaps (halftime) must not crash the missed-play search; any scan crash shows in the status pill.
7. Lead-in on a head-on close-up (non-field shot) that cuts to the wide field view before the snap → start at that cut, at any distance before the snap (no minimum; a cut up to 0.2s after the snap counts; start never later than the snap) — FSU play 6 opened on the QB close-up; play 73 (Q4 1:58) opened on a kicker stat graphic because its cut was 0.38s before the snap, under the old 0.4s floor.
6. Penalty sign: ESPN text uses its own team codes (Miami = "UM", not "MIA") — map code→team from spot vs "from UM06" before deciding − (on offense) or + (on defense) — FSU play 25 false start showed +3. Codes can be 2–6 letters (Miami = "CANES" vs Pitt, Pitt = "PIT" not "PITT") — Pitt play 1 false start showed +5.
8. Two plays can't share one clip — fix immediately, never just flag: if a play's snap lands before/on the previous play's (+8s) and no wide set→snap is found, fall back to the next detected motion snap — FSU plays 29/30 were the same clip (play 30 snap sat 1.6s before play 29's).
9. Self-check runs on every load, including cached scans (it was only running after a fresh scan, so cached games showed no red dots).
10. Play still not found (e.g. first play of a quarter after the break) → back-solve the snap from the running scoreboard clock (read time − (start clock − read clock), ≥3 agreeing reads; start clock = frozen pre-snap reading, else 15:00 at quarter start — ESPN truncates, Q4 "14:59" was 15:00, put the snap 1s late; clock shows floor → intersect reads) — FSU clip 56 (Q4 14:59) was "not found".
11. Snap hidden by the broadcast (graphic/close-up until <0.6s before the snap, play already moving when the field returns) → can't be fixed: start once the dissolve has fully faded in (green level at its plateau — may be just after the snap), never on the dissolve. No red dot (user rejected it). Never report this as fixed — FSU play 73 (Q4 1:58): the kicker stat graphic covered the snap.
12. Scoreboard down &amp; distance at the snap must match ESPN's (down mismatch, or D&amp;D mismatch + clock &gt;10s off, or clock &gt;25s off, or frozen clock on a live play, or &lt;6s after previous clip, or running broadcast clock &gt;6s above ESPN's at the snap (clip before the snap), or &gt;3s below it (clip after the snap); broadcast clock at a correct snap reads 0–6s above ESPN) → search where the scoreboard shows ESPN's clock (±12s, same quarter; ESPN drifts 2–6s from the broadcast clock) for a snap (wide set→motion, clock frozen→running, or back-solve from running clock) whose D&amp;D matches. A misplaced previous clip must not bound the search. Runs before the dupe fix — Pitt Q1: punt clip sat before the snap (clock frozen), then one missed snap after a false start (play 6, 1st &amp; 15) shifted every later clip one play late; Q3 opened ~80s off. Offense play 56 (Q3 9:34, after a false start, clock restarted on ready) ended in the huddle: D&amp;D matched but clock read 9:45.
13. A clip confirmed by the scoreboard (D&amp;D + clock match ESPN) is never moved by a later fix. On an order conflict the unconfirmed neighbour moves. A red dot means every fix was tried and failed — never flag what can still be fixed. — Pitt Q3: one misplaced pre-snap penalty clip (play 94) made the overlap fix push ~30 correct clips 6+ min late.
14. Kick tab label varies by broadcast: Pitt (ESPN) shows the ESPN logo in the down &amp; distance tab on PATs/kickoffs, not "KICKOFF"/"PAT" → logo counts as a kick label. Kicks are re-found after rule 12 moves their neighbours (kick left before its TD / after next snap) — Pitt: 0 of 20 kicks found.
15. Re-running the snap check (fixDone bump) must also re-run rule 12 (ddDone reset) — the bump wiped rule-12 fixes but ddDone stayed set, so they were never redone: Pitt went from fixed to 43 flags.
16. A play right before a try is a TD → ESPN's clock is the clock AFTER the play, even when the text leads with "(13:00)"; clock frozen right after the snap is expected, not "not a live play" — Pitt play 97 (Q3 13:00) was moved onto play 100. STILL OPEN: 97 still lands on 100 after this.
17. TD play (ESPN clock = END clock): the snap must sit ≥ 2s + 1s per 7 yds (3–12s) before the scoreboard stops on ESPN's time; a clock "start" needs 3 equal reads before it (one duplicate read on a running clock is not a stop); if the previous play stopped the clock (incomplete, flag, out of bounds) the snap is where the clock starts running from that play's clock — Clemson play 6 (Q1 13:05, 61-yd TD) started at 13:09, 8s after the snap.
18. The scoreboard-first flow had no repair pass (ddFix only ran in the old flow): after placement, a play whose scoreboard at the snap reads >3s below ESPN's clock, or >8s above it on a running clock, or that shares a clip with a neighbour, is re-found within ±150s where the scoreboard shows ESPN's clock (frozen→running, else back-solve), never on top of a confirmed neighbour; confirmed clips don't move — Clemson 105/106 shared one clip (105 sat 20s late); also 85/86, 88/89, 128/129, 133/134.
19. Stopped-clock search keeps going past commercial breaks until the scoreboard has run past ESPN's time (it stopped at the first 20s gap in reads) — Clemson play 33 (Q1 3:11, after FG + kickoff + break) was "not found".
21. A placed snap with no scoreboard on screen anywhere in ±3s is on a graphic/close-up/break, not the play → repair it like rule 18 (re-find where the scoreboard shows ESPN's clock) — Clemson offense play 03 (Q1 9:38, first play after a punt + break) sat on the Mensah season-stats graphic ~3 min early.
22. The scoreboard passes may only move a clip if the scoreboard backs the new spot better than the first (wallclock) placement: clock stopped before + running after the snap, clock in ESPN's 0–7s window, scoreboard on screen; a TD whose clock already shows ESPN's end clock is after the play. Never revert onto another clip — Clemson offense play 02 (Q1 13:05 TD) was right for a few seconds after every load, then the passes moved it 10s late. UPDATE 2: neither placement is right everywhere — Clemson's first placement was right in Q1 (offense 02, 03) and ~4 min late through Q2 (offense 18, 19), the scoreboard's the reverse. Per play, score both by the clock right before the snap (normal play: ESPN −1…+7; TD: previous stopping play's clock −2…+1, else ≥3s above the end clock; no scoreboard in ±3s = −1); the higher score wins, a tie keeps the scoreboard's. (The first evidence version tied on 02: the clock had just started, so no 'stopped before' was seen.)
23. After a play that stops the clock (punt, kick, turnover, incompletion, out of bounds, flag, score, timeout) the clock stays stopped until the next snap. If the scoreboard runs without a stop from the previous snap to this clip, the clip is still inside the previous play → move it to where the clock next starts from ESPN's time (same quarter). After a change of possession (punt, kickoff, turnover) the next snap is never within 25s of it, and never before it. This is the one scoreboard move allowed over the first placement — Clemson offense play 19 (Q2 11:36, after Clemson's punt) sat on the punt return; offense 22 (Q2 6:12) 8s after a punt; offense 29 (Q3 14:55) 5s after the 2nd-half kickoff; defense Q1 13:02 sat before Miami's kickoff; defense Q3 1:50 10s after a kickoff.
24. ROOT FIX (tested offline on the whole Clemson game): one clock check per play, then targeted fixes — finalize() in app.html. A play is right when the scoreboard clock just before its snap reads ESPN −1…+8 (≥3s of reads around it, not frozen through 8s, not inside the previous play's run); TDs/end-clock plays must start where the clock started running before it stopped on ESPN's time. Failing plays take the first placement if that passes, else the first clock start at their time between good neighbours (never within 12s of another clip). Kicks go after their score and before the next snap. Findings: the first (wallclock) placement drifts a play late from Q1 ~11:55 on (132 of 150 fail); the scoreboard placement failed 13; after finalize 4 remain (one OCR misread 0:51→0:57, one 2:03 misread, two without scoreboard). Kick-type plays are found by type/result, never by text — every TD's text contains "kick attempt", which had hidden all TDs from the checks.
    Replay harness: grey circle in the app saves play-clipper-debug.json (scan + scoreboard reads + ESPN game, no video) → `node tools/replay.js <file>` then `python3 tools/check.py fin|raw|base <file>` lists failing plays. Use this before shipping any clip change; never patch clip-by-clip from screenshots.
25. A moved clip gets a fresh lead-in (start at the camera cut into the field shot if one falls in the 3s before the snap, else snap −3s; rules 1/7), and no clip opens on a crowd/close-up/graphic when a field shot starts before the snap — moved clips had kept their old 3s offset. Clemson offense 19 (Q2 11:36): snap right, but the clip opened on a fan close-up 0.7s before the cut. Checked offline: clips opening off the field 23 → 16 (the rest have no field shot before the snap: kicks, rule 11).
26. A cached game shows no clips until its saved scoreboard placement is loaded (≤15s fallback). Showing the first (wallclock) placement first made clips look right, then 'jump', and their previews blank out and come back as thumbnails were redone for the new starts.
27. Overturned review: ESPN appends the reversed call as "(Original Play: …)" — drop it before anything reads the text (dropOverturned in fetchGame). Clemson offense play 12 (Q1 4:14 incomplete) showed a TD star + sack and got a fake extra point from the overturned sack-fumble-TD.
28. A TD only counts if it stood: "TOUCHDOWN nullified by penalty … NO PLAY" or a TD call overturned on review is not a TD (hasTD) — it gets a "TD called back" tag/flag instead (tdBack; search "td called back"). Never test /touchdown/ on raw text. Clemson offense play 27 (Q2 4:19, Toney 58-yd TD wiped by illegal shift) showed as a TD in search.
20. The viewer never reviews clips: no red dots, no "to review" button. Shift+Option+D copies a per-play table (clip times + scoreboard reads + self-check flags) for debugging.
Audit flags: not found on video · overlaps next play · clock frozen · wrong quarter on scoreboard · scoreboard clock >20s from ESPN.

# Deploying
- The app is app.html; index.html is a tiny loader that reads version.json (no cache) and opens app.html?v=<build>, because GitHub Pages serves cached HTML for up to 10 min (the user kept getting old builds after Cmd+Shift+R).
- Run ./bump.sh before every push (build number in app.html + version.json). Self-update is OFF (watchForUpdates not called): the user doesn't want the page reloading on its own. New versions load on the user's next reload.
